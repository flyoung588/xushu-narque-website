01 / AIOS

Position for the Critical Junctions of the Machine Economy

As intelligence and digital capabilities become more abundant, value will not necessarily remain in easily replicated software. Energy, grid access, productive capacity, industrial location, unique data, and legally effective rights may become critical constraints—and sources of capital value.

02 / AIOS

Three Ways of Reasoning About the Same Investment World

01 ↗

Future backcasting

Start from multiple plausible machine-economy futures and infer necessary changes in technology, production, and resources.

02 ↗

Reality-forward analysis

Start from observed scientific, industrial, corporate, policy, and infrastructure changes to identify emerging economic mechanisms.

03 ↗

Price-implied reasoning

Start from securities and asset prices to examine expectations already reflected by markets, along with neglected risks and changes.

03 / AIOS

From Economic Change to the Rightful Beneficiary

A technical breakthrough or resource shortage does not automatically create an investable return. AIOS examines who pays, how full costs change, which parties hold scarce rights, how much economic surplus companies can retain, and how those changes may be reflected in capital prices.

05 / AIOS

A Continuous Research Process, Not a One-Time Forecast

AIOS considers competing futures, real-world evidence, economic value, ownership and contractual rights, and market prices in a shared research framework. New information can alter the thesis, the alternatives, the risks, and the opportunity cost of capital.

06 / AIOS

Science and Capital Research Inform Each Other

XREM may expand technological and productive possibilities. AIOS may reveal scientific bottlenecks through its analysis of industries, resources, and investment opportunities. METRE provides a shared theoretical language, and real-world evidence helps all three refine their questions.
